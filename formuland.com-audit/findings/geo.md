## What works

- **Server-side rendering.** Every saved page carries its full text in the raw HTML: product description, ingredients, nutrition values, FAQ answers and Judge.me reviews in Product JSON-LD. AI crawlers do not need to run JavaScript to see the content.
- **Explicit allow group for AI retrieval bots.** robots.txt has a commented group for ChatGPT-User, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Claude-User and Claude-SearchBot.
- **The HiPP collection is the best-structured page for citation.** https://formuland.com/collections/hipp-formula has:
  - an HTML `<table>` with `<th>` headers (41 rows × 7 columns: Formula, Line, Type, Stage, Age, Milk, Size) plus a Stage/Key Notes table;
  - 5 question-shaped headings, e.g. "Which HiPP Formula Stage Does My Baby Need?";
  - a link to the FDA list;
  - a model YMYL answer, "Is HiPP formula FDA approved?" (114 words). It says the FDA does not "approve" formula, that HiPP is not on the FDA's list of formulas marketed in the U.S., and cites Regulation (EU) 2016/127.
- **The recall page cites primary sources.** https://formuland.com/pages/nara-organics-formula-recall links to the FDA recall notice, a CDC release, Cleveland Clinic and CIDRAP. It has 9 H2s and a 5-column UPC/lot table.
- **FAQ answers are short and direct.**
  - https://formuland.com/pages/formuland-faqs has 27 Q&As in FAQPage JSON-LD, most answering in the first sentence.
  - It includes a dated first-party statistic: "98% of these orders arrived within 4 business days (orders placed September–October 2026)".
  - 48 of 74 collections also carry FAQPage (raw/crawl-summary.json).
- **Organization JSON-LD is on every page**, with `foundingDate` 2014, 5 sameAs links (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, Trustpilot) and a ContactPoint. crawl-summary.json reports 0 invalid JSON-LD blocks.

## Findings

### F1. YMYL factual errors and contradictions in a flagship buying-guide article
Severity: **Critical**

Evidence: https://formuland.com/blogs/parenting-tips/best-organic-baby-formula-which-is-safest-for-your-child (3,093 words; Article schema `dateModified` 2026-10-29).
- It says: "feeding your baby organic formula is the best option, since there will be zero chance of GMOs, no pesticides and no DHA/ARA".
- Under the DHA/ARA heading it says: "Avoid these whenever possible."
- The site contradicts this in three places:
  - The HiPP UK Organic Stage 1 product page lists "Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA/omega-3) 13.2mg" and "Arachidonic acid (AA) 13.2mg" per 100 ml.
  - The HiPP collection FAQ says HiPP Organic is "formulated with key nutrients including DHA".
  - The same article later says "Check for key nutrients like DHA (for brain development)".
- The site itself cites Regulation (EU) 2016/127, which makes DHA mandatory in EU infant formula.
- The article recommends Jovie and says "We offer a selection of the best organic infant formulas, including Holle, Jovie, and Lebenswert". Jovie appears 0 times in sitemap_products_1.xml (317 URLs) and 0 times in llms.txt.
- The H3 "Pros and Cons of Jovie Organic Formula" appears twice.
- "HiPP Organic has been producing baby food since the 1930s" conflicts with the collection page's "made food for babies since 1899".
- The visible date says "Updated May 27, 2026", but schema says 2026-10-29.

A second article, https://formuland.com/blogs/parenting-tips/what-to-feed-your-infant (2016-09-23; modified 2025-04-21), advises that "you should do away with foods which are most likely to cause allergies". That conflicts with current US guidance on early allergen introduction (the NIAID 2017 peanut addendum).

AI engines cross-check health claims against authorities and against the site's own pages. Contradictions like these lower the trust score for the whole domain on YMYL queries.

Recommendation:
- Rewrite the DHA/ARA, palm-oil and carrageenan sections, citing EU 2016/127, the FDA and AAP/healthychildren.org.
- Remove or replace the Jovie recommendations and the duplicate H3.
- Align the HiPP founding fact (1899).
- Rewrite the allergen advice to match current guidance.
- Have a named pediatric dietitian or pediatrician review both articles. Show "Medically reviewed by [name, credential] on [date]" and set `reviewedBy` in the schema.
- Verify: grep both URLs for "no DHA/ARA", "Jovie" and "do away with foods". All three should return 0 hits.

### F2. The European Baby Formula Comparison Chart is image-only
Severity: **High**

Evidence:
- https://formuland.com/pages/european-baby-formula-comparison-chart has 5 words of text in `<main>` (crawl.jsonl `words: 5`), 0 `<table>` elements and 0 H2s.
- The chart is 3 images with generic alt text: "European baby formula comparison chart, part 1/2/3". Image version timestamps decode to 2024-09-04 and 2024-10-29.
- Part 1 (viewed) shows 26 HiPP rows × 15 attributes: organic, prebiotic, probiotic, starch, maltodextrin, palm oil, DHA and so on. That is about 390 data points that exist only as pixels.
- It has no HiPP PUR row, although the sitemap lists 5 HiPP PUR products.
- Meta description and llms.txt promise prebiotic/probiotic/starch/maltodextrin/palm-oil coverage that text extraction cannot reach.
- This is the site's most citable asset for "which European formula has no palm oil / maltodextrin" questions.

Recommendation:
- Rebuild the chart as an HTML `<table>`, one per brand, with `<caption>`, `<th scope="col">` and Yes/No text in each cell (not icons only).
- Add a short definitional intro of about 50 words, a visible "Last verified: [date]" with the label sources, and the missing lines (HiPP PUR, Kendamil Bio Nature).
- Keep the images as a downloadable graphic with descriptive alt text.
- Verify: `curl` the page and confirm that `<table` appears and that "maltodextrin" appears more than 25 times as text.

### F3. No real authorship, reviewer or expert entities on YMYL content
Severity: **High**

Evidence:
- The Article JSON-LD on both articles has `"author": {"@type": "Person", "name": "Formuland"}`. crawl-summary.json counts 111 Article pages, all on this template.
- No visible byline or reviewer appears on the saved articles.
- The About page (https://formuland.com/pages/about-us) says "We work with pediatric and nutritional experts" but names no one. It also names no founders, legal name or street address; the only location is "Founded in 2014 in California".
- The best-organic article has 1 external link (statista.com) and 0 links to health authorities. "Research has shown..." and "palm oil has been shown to lower calcium..." have no citations.

Recommendation:
- Create author and reviewer profile pages with credentials and photos.
- In schema, use `author` as a real Person (with `jobTitle`, `sameAs` to LinkedIn) and add `reviewedBy`. Keep the publisher as the Organization.
- Name the "pediatric and nutritional experts" on the About page.
- Add primary-source citations inline (FDA, EFSA, EU regulation, AAP).
- Verify: the Rich Results Test or `parse_html.py` should show the Article `author.@type` as Person with a real name.

### F4. Cloudflare managed challenge on automated traffic; AI bot reachability unconfirmed
Severity: **Medium**

Evidence:
- 32 of 500 crawl URLs returned 429 "Verifying your connection..." at polite crawl rates. This is a Cloudflare managed challenge (`cType: 'managed'`). All 32 still returned 429 on retry (raw/crawl-retry.jsonl: 32×429, 10×200).
- raw/pages/blogs_parenting_tips.html (9,370 bytes) is the challenge page, so the blog index was never captured.
- Two rounds of user-agent tests were run on 2026-11-02 between 18:58 and 19:01 UTC, from a datacenter IP (server-timing `asn 396982`):
  - Run 1: GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, CCBot and Googlebot got 200. ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, bingbot and the audit UA got a 403 challenge.
  - Run 2 (reverse order, 4 s spacing, /pages/formuland-faqs): everything got 403, including a desktop Chrome UA, except the Googlebot and CCBot UA strings.
- These tests spoof UAs from an unverified IP, so they cannot show how verified bots coming from their published IP ranges are treated. Still, the challenge is aggressive, and JS challenges cannot be solved by AI fetchers.

Recommendation:
- Ask Shopify support to confirm that verified bots (bingbot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot) are exempt from the storefront bot challenge.
- Check Bing Webmaster Tools URL Inspection for article URLs.
- Watch server-side or Shopify analytics bot logs for 403/429 responses to AI user agents.
- Verify: Bing URL Inspection shows "fetched successfully" for /blogs/parenting-tips/why-european-baby-formula-comes-in-stages, which was one of the challenged URLs.

### F5. Entity signals are thin and inconsistent
Severity: **Medium**

Evidence:
- The Organization node has no `legalName`, `address`, `telephone`, `founder`, or YouTube, LinkedIn or Wikidata in `sameAs`. The Facebook sameAs carries a `?_rdr` tracking parameter.
- /pages/about-us and /pages/our-company both emit AboutPage nodes named "About Us – Formuland". They also emit a second Organization node with the same `@id` but different content: no `foundingDate`, and no Trustpilot in sameAs.
- Claims conflict across pages:
  - "Trusted by 1M+ Families" (about-us) vs "thousands of families" (our-company).
  - "serving parents and caregivers for 10 years" (the formuland-reviews description in llms.txt) vs "Since 2014", which is 12 years.
- Wikipedia and Wikidata presence: not measured (both APIs returned 429).

Recommendation:
- Use one canonical About page and 301 or canonicalize the other to it.
- Emit a single Organization node site-wide with `legalName` (the Facebook page title suggests "Formuland Inc"; confirm it), postal `address`, `founder` Person(s), `telephone`, and sameAs links to LinkedIn, YouTube and Trustpilot. Strip the query string from the Facebook URL.
- Reconcile the family count and the years in business.
- Create a Wikidata item once independent sources exist.
- Verify: `parse_html.py` shows exactly one Organization node per page.

### F6. Key pages lack headings, dates and descriptions
Severity: **Medium**

Evidence:
- /pages/formuland-faqs has 0 H1 and 0 H2/H3 in HTML. The 27 questions are accordion labels, not headings. crawl-summary.json `h1_missing` also lists the FAQs, about-us and the Nara recall page.
- The recall page is time-sensitive safety content. It has no `datePublished`/`dateModified` (only Organization and WebSite schema), no meta description and a blank llms.txt description.
- what-to-feed-your-infant uses `<span style="font-size: 18pt;">` for its section titles, so the article has no real H2s.

Recommendation:
- Add an H1 to each of these pages.
- Mark each FAQ question as an `<h3>` inside the accordion.
- Give the recall page Article schema with dates, a visible "Last updated" line and a meta description.
- Convert the styled spans to `<h2>`.
- Verify: re-run the crawl. `h1_missing` should not list these URLs.

### F7. Auto-generated llms.txt is uncurated and carries agent directives
Severity: **Low**

Evidence:
- It is 237,834 bytes, which is a catalog dump rather than a curated index.
- The summary is promotional ("Founded by parents for parents, discover...") and omits who the company is (an importer/retailer, founded 2014, California).
- It lists 200 of the 317 sitemap products.
- Utility pages are included with blank descriptions: quiz-results, froonze-new-customer-account, customer-portal, data-sharing-opt-out.
- The FAQs, recall, about-us and our-company entries are blank.
- It opens with an "Agent Instructions" section addressed to AI agents (Shop skill, UCP endpoints). That is platform boilerplate; it was reported here and not acted on.

Recommendation:
- If Shopify allows a custom llms.txt, publish a curated one of about 5 KB. It should give a factual summary and list the comparison chart, FAQs, the HiPP/Kendamil/Holle collection guides, the recall page and policies, with one-line descriptions.
- If an override is not possible, write meta descriptions for the blank pages; those feed the auto-generated entries.

### F8. Main content sits deep in heavy HTML
Severity: **Low**

Evidence:
- `<main>` starts at byte 230,965 to 323,996 on all 7 key pages measured.
- Visible text is 1.8% of HTML on the homepage, 3.5% on the HiPP Stage 1 product and 3.2% on the HiPP collection. Script and style account for 462K to 695K characters.
- Any fetcher with a byte cap under about 230 KB sees no content.

Recommendation:
- Defer or trim inline app JSON and scripts in `<head>` (Judge.me, GWP and other app blobs).
- Verify: the byte offset of `<main` falls below 100 KB.

### F9. Product pages use the manufacturer's voice and split Product entities
Severity: **Low**

Evidence:
- /products/hipp-formula-stage-1 copies HiPP's first-person copy, e.g. "Our new tin is fully recyclable". That gives AI engines no reason to cite Formuland over hipp.co.uk.
- The page emits two Product nodes: one with offers and no rating, the other with `aggregateRating` 4.91/107 and no offers. Neither has a GTIN. The brand is given as "Hipp".
- Nutrition values are text lines, not a table.

Recommendation:
- Add a Formuland-authored, 130 to 170 word "Who this is for / how it differs from HiPP Dutch and German" block.
- Merge into one Product node with a GTIN and brand "HiPP".
- Render the nutrition values as a `<table>`.

## What AI engines need before citing a YMYL infant-nutrition retailer

- Named, credentialed reviewers.
- Claims consistent with FDA, EU 2016/127 and AAP guidance, and with the site's own product data.
- Dated, sourced safety content.
- A verifiable legal entity (address, legal name, founders).
- Independent corroboration (Wikidata, press, Reddit, YouTube).
- Clear statements on import status: not on the FDA list of formulas marketed in the U.S. The HiPP collection FAQ already models this well.
